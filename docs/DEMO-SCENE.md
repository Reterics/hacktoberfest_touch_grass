# Moss Demo Scene — Open Innovation

## Demo Scene: “Moss Needs a Tree”

The demo should prove the whole idea in about **60–90 seconds**, rather than simply showing Moss sitting on the desk.

Start in the existing room. Moss is sitting beside the laptop, slightly bored. The focus timer reaches the end of a work session. His fern fronds slowly droop and the room becomes a little less lively.

A small message appears:

> **Moss:** “We’ve been staring at this screen long enough.”  
> **Quest:** Find me a tree 🌳  
> **Reward:** +15 XP · Ecosystem growth

The player picks up their phone and walks outside. The phone view opens directly from the game and asks them to photograph a tree.

They take the photo.

The local **Gemma** vision/model pipeline recognizes that there is a tree in the image. The phone gives a small confirmation:

> **Nature found. ✓**

Cut immediately back to the desktop scene.

Moss’s fern fronds spring upward. He does a small happy animation. Light comes through the window. A little leaf blows into the room and lands on the desk.

The plant beside Moss grows slightly.

The ecosystem meter advances:

**● ● ○ ○ ○ ○**

Moss says something deadpan rather than overly cute:

> “Acceptable tree.”

Then:

**+15 XP**  
**New discovery added to Moss’s ecosystem**

The player presses:

**Start focus — 45:00**

Moss sits down next to his tiny laptop again.

This single sequence communicates almost the entire product:

**work → interruption → real world → AI understanding → game consequence → back to work**

That is the core magic of the concept.

## Why Open Innovation Matters

Moss should not describe open innovation as simply:

> “We use open source.”

The stronger idea is:

> **Moss exists because different technologies can be combined into something none of them were originally designed to build.**

The experience combines local AI, computer vision, web technology, phones, 3D/game technology, and potentially community-created models and assets into one product.

That is what open innovation enables.

Instead of waiting for one company to create an “AI-powered outdoor coworker Tamagotchi,” we can take the best building blocks from an ecosystem and invent the experience ourselves.

### Building Blocks

- **Gemma** provides local intelligence and vision capabilities.
- **Three.js / WebGL** provides the interactive 3D world.
- **The phone** becomes a real-world sensor and camera.
- **Open web standards** connect devices and experiences.
- **Community models and tools** accelerate AI, 3D, animation, voice, and experimentation.

Because the architecture is not dependent on one proprietary black box, Moss can keep evolving.

Today:

> “Show me a tree.”

Tomorrow:

> “Find something yellow.”  
> “Walk somewhere with grass.”  
> “Identify this plant.”  
> “Go somewhere you can hear birds.”  
> “Photograph something you haven’t discovered before.”

Different models and open technologies can specialize in different parts of that experience.

---

## Why It Matters for Small Teams

Open innovation is not valuable only because the technology is open.

It matters because:

> **It lets a small team build experiences that previously required several specialized teams.**

AI, game technology, computer vision, device connectivity, 3D tools, and community ecosystems can be composed into one product far faster than building every layer from scratch.

That changes what a small team is capable of inventing.

---

## Demo Message

A simple presentation message could be:

> ## Open innovation turns building blocks into experiences.
>
> Local AI understands the world.  
> Open technologies connect devices.  
> Community tools accelerate creation.  
> **We combine them into something new: an AI companion that gets you away from AI.**

The final line captures the central contradiction of Moss:

> **We are using AI to make people spend less time staring at computers.**

That is more memorable than presenting the project as simply a local LLM integration.
